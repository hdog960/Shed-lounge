## Findings (with official/source links)

### 1. State electrical and residential codes
- Indiana Department of Homeland Security lists **2020 Indiana Residential Code** (2018 IRC with Indiana amendments) as in force, and **2009 Indiana Electrical Code** (based on 2008 NEC, with Indiana amendments) as in force. A new edition has been contemplated, so always re-check at permit date.
- Indiana Residential Code **675 IAC 14-4.4-12** adds section R309.6 and Table R309 for detached garages and accessory structures. For a qualifying **portable accessory structure 200 sq ft maximum**, the table states **one 20-amp electrical circuit** and no permanent heat or water/sanitation. This does NOT establish that our existing shed is portable or may be occupied as planned.
- R309.6 states that *any habitable area* in a detached accessory structure must meet all applicable sections, including an exit door/window under R310/R311. The Indiana definition of **habitable space** includes space for living, sleeping, eating or cooking; a regularly used recreational lounge needs classification guidance, not assumptions.
- Interior shed footprint is about 61 sq ft but **exterior footprint and foundation/support type are not verified**.

### 2. Local permitting and licensing
- Evansville municipal code **15.05.210(A)** requires electrical work to conform to Indiana Electrical Code.
- Vanderburgh County **15.08.030** generally requires permits before regulated installations/changes; even its low-cost repair exception **explicitly excludes installation, alteration and replacement of electrical wiring and service panels**.
- Evansville **5.70.040** addresses trade licensing. Evansville **5.70.110** provides a **temporary homeowner contractor** route for some owner/family work under conditions and inspector review; it is NOT blanket permission for unqualified or unpermitted new shed wiring. Confirm eligibility with the building department.
- If conversion amounts to a regulated change of use, an Improvement Location Permit (ILP) may be needed under City **18.170.010**; the local inspector/APC must determine applicability.

### 3. Zoning location, foundation and occupancy
- Evansville City **18.130.070** defines accessory structures and includes setback and easement rules. Its special no-utilities rule for accessory structures on an otherwise undeveloped adjacent lot is NOT a universal prohibition on power to backyard sheds on the same lot.
- Vanderburgh County accessory zoning rules differ in detail; the correct city/county location matters.
- A recreational lounge is not automatically a separate dwelling. However, the building office must determine if regularly occupied use triggers habitable-space, exit, lighting, safety or other requirements.
- Check whether the parcel is subject to floodplain rules before modifying or electrifying.

### 4. Digging / underground utilities
Indiana's Call Before You Dig law requires **811 contact at least two full working days before excavation**. No route, trench depth, conduit type, conductor size, GFCI/disconnect arrangements, or grading is approved by this research; ask electrician and inspector. Privately owned buried lines may require additional private locating.

IURC official page: https://www.in.gov/iurc/pipeline-safety-division/regulation-of-gas-operators/call-before-you-dig-law/  
Indiana811: https://indiana811.org/homeowners/5-steps-to-safe-digging/

## Specific questions to ask BEFORE purchasing electrical materials
1. We have a small existing detached backyard shed. **Does it qualify as a portable 200-sq-ft accessory structure under R309/Table R309?**
2. For occasional two-person lounge/recreation use (no sleeping, kitchen, toilet, or dwelling), **is this considered habitable space or a regulated change of use?** What exit/ventilation rules follow?
3. If we bring permanent electrical power to the shed, **is one 120V 20A branch circuit the applicable maximum?** Can it have multiple receptacles and a switched LED?
4. What **electrical permit, zoning/ILP and inspections** are required for an already-existing shed in this location?
5. Is the **homeowner permit** route available for new wiring to a detached structure, or must a city/county licensed electrician pull and execute it?
6. What rules govern **underground routing, GFCI protection, disconnection, grounding and exterior penetrations**?
7. Are there restrictions on **ventilation, a window, portable cooling, heating, insulation**, especially if it qualifies as portable?
8. How should we evaluate a potential **floodplain or setback/easement** issue, given the shed already exists?

## Cautions
- A nominal 20A × 120V = 2,400W is not the same as unlimited available continuous usable power; circuit loading and dedicated-load decisions require electrical design.
- Do not permanently power shed via yard extension cord.
- No interior wiring, trenching or insulation should begin before the jurisdiction, building classification, permit process and structure condition are verified.
- Keep private property address, photos, panel details and any actual underground route out of the public repo.
