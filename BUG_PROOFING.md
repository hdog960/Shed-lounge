# Bug Resistance — Survey and Seal Plan
Updated 2026-11-10. **Status: inspection and sealing not yet done.**

## Goal
Make the shed consistently comfortable with very few insect intrusions while keeping it dry and correctly ventilated. Absolute bug elimination is not realistic.

## First survey
Inspect **both sides of the door**, threshold, floor perimeter, all floor seams, wall-to-roof joints, roof edge, visible daylight, existing vents, electrical penetrations (if any), corners behind/under fixed shelves and under the structure. Look for damp staining, droppings, nesting, cobwebs and gaps.

## Repair order
1. Fix roof leaks, standing water or dampness; don't trap moisture behind interior finishes.
2. Install a tight fitting door sweep + threshold and perimeter weatherstripping; verify door still closes and latches.
3. Use suitable sealants/materials at **unintended** gaps and around penetrations; use pest-resistant barriers for larger holes.
4. Fit insect screens to actual vents without blocking airflow; avoid sealing required roof/attic ventilation.
5. Remove exterior leaf litter, debris, overgrown vegetation and moisture attractants around shed.
6. Recheck after rain, at dusk and again a week later.

## Notes
- Existing exposed wood/OSB surfaces require moisture assessment before insulation or coverings.
- Do not spray pesticides casually throughout an eventual occupied lounge.
- If pests are already nesting inside, diagnose and treat the source rather than merely sealing them in.
- A later **screened exhaust** is important if smoke will be generated indoors.

## Unknowns
- Condition of lower floor and supports
- Door gaps and current threshold quality
- Type of rear roof vent/opening seen in interior photo
- Whether exterior bushes/trees affect roof moisture or pest paths
